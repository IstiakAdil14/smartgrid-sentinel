We selected the Informer model for production deployment over the GRU network, despite the latter's slightly higher raw accuracy (87.69% vs. 87.41%). The GRU model exhibited severe overfitting after 20 epochs, as evidenced by a diverging validation loss. In contrast, the Informer model converged stably across all 50 epochs (refer to Fig. 2). Furthermore, the Informer achieved a higher Weighted F1-score (88.44% compared to 88.39% for the GRU) and showed better predictive capability on the minority Medium-Risk class, reaching 79.00% recall compared to the GRU's 73.00%.

[Figure: Informer vs. GRU Training and Validation Loss Curves.]

Although the Random Forest model achieved a marginally higher Macro F1-score than the Informer (77.87% versus 77.68%, a difference of 0.19%), it remains unsuitable for actual grid deployment. Standard ensemble classifiers cannot capture sequential temporal dependencies or handle multi-step forecasting horizons without complex engineering. The Informer natively models chronological sequence dynamics through self-attention, making it much better suited for scale and multi-node grid expansions.
